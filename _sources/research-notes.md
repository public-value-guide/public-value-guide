## Grounding

The book's spine is public value theory: Mark Moore's strategic triangle (legitimacy and support, value proposition, operational capacity), from *Creating Public Value: Strategic Management in Government* (1995) and *Recognizing Public Value* (2013). Extensions and companions to weave through: John Benington's and Mark Moore's edited volume *Public Value: Theory and Practice* (2011); Gerry Stoker's "public value management" as a successor paradigm to New Public Management; Stephen Osborne's New Public Governance; Barry Bozeman's public-value mapping and his work on publicness; John Alford and Janine O'Flynn's critical engagement with the strategic triangle; John Bryson, Barbara Crosby, and Laura Bloomberg's "Public Value Governance" synthesis.

Standing institutional sources to draw on across chapters, per `spec/index.md` §6: OECD (especially its public governance and open government work), World Bank (public sector governance indicators), UN agencies (UNDP, UN DESA on public administration), national audit and accountability offices (UK National Audit Office, US Government Accountability Office, and equivalents elsewhere), national and regional open-government/open-data portals, and peer-reviewed public-administration journals (*Public Administration Review*, *Public Management Review*, *Journal of Public Administration Research and Theory*).
